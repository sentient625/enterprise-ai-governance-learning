## Scope and learning claim

The package includes the lesson, participant workbook, model answer/review guide, source-register update, and navigation/content-register changes. It develops executive and program-lead judgment about AI-specific vendor due diligence, requiring an AI bill of materials as a concrete artifact rather than a marketing claim, building a six-control contract-control checklist, treating vendor non-disclosure as a governed risk finding, and designing ongoing (not just onboarding-time) vendor governance.

Publication does not establish learner completion, production readiness, independent peer review, or professional authority. The due-diligence specification, contract-control checklist, and fictional case are original educational synthesis, not a NIST-, CISA-, ISO-, or OMB-prescribed procurement form or legal contract template.

## Limitations and maintenance

This module does not itself specify legal contract language, liability allocation, or indemnification terms; those are reserved to qualified legal review. The fictional case's retroactive due-diligence reconstruction is illustrative; learners must preserve the case's own honestly acknowledged gaps (an unverified change-disclosure clause, an untested exit plan) rather than treating the model answer's recommendations as a resolved outcome. OMB M-25-22 is federal-sector-specific and illustrative only; it must not be represented as binding on a private-sector or non-U.S.-government learner's organization. The CISA/G7 AI-BOM guidance is cited for its general framing and cluster structure, not as a mandated format this module requires learners to reproduce exactly.

Review again when source status changes (particularly further CISA/G7 AI-BOM guidance revisions, given how recently it was published, or further OMB AI-memo activity), when a later planned module needs this module's contract-control checklist tightened, or when reader feedback identifies accuracy, usability, confidentiality, or positioning concerns. Confirm direct publisher-page access for the sources listed above at the next revision opportunity, since this release relied on search-based corroboration rather than a direct fetch.
