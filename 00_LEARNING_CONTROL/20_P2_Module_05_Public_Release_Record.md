## Scope and learning claim

The package includes the lesson, participant workbook, model answer/review guide, source-register update, and navigation/content-register changes. It develops executive and program-lead judgment about specifying an ongoing monitoring plan with real thresholds, owners, and response deadlines; distinguishing a leading indicator from a confirmed incident; defining a material-change envelope; and requiring vendor and third-party change disclosure with an honestly acknowledged fallback when disclosure fails.

Publication does not establish learner completion, production readiness, independent peer review, or professional authority. The monitoring specification, change-governance model, and fictional case are original educational synthesis, not a NIST-, ISO-, or vendor-prescribed monitoring architecture.

## Limitations and maintenance

This module does not itself specify vendor and supply-chain due-diligence practice beyond the single disclosure-term illustration; broader vendor governance is reserved for a later planned module. The fictional case's monitoring plan and six-months-later scenario are illustrative; learners must preserve the case's honestly acknowledged gap (an untested vendor-disclosure obligation) rather than treating the added contract term as a solved problem. This module's change-envelope example is specific to the benefits-eligibility case and must not be treated as a generic template applicable without re-deriving the boundary from a given use case's own actual failure modes.

Review again when source status changes (particularly ISO/IEC 42001-family or NIST AI RMF revision activity), when the next planned module on vendor and supply-chain governance is drafted and this module's disclosure-term forward reference needs tightening, or when reader feedback identifies accuracy, usability, confidentiality, or positioning concerns.
