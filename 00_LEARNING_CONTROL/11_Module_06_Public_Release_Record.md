## Release scope

This decision applies to the Module 6 lesson, participant workbook, model answer and review guide, source/terminology additions, and navigation/control updates.

It does not certify learner competence, prescribe production architecture, or provide a legal, compliance, audit, privacy, safety, labor, regulatory, or professional determination.

## Learning claim

The package develops program-level fluency sufficient to:

- distinguish participation, oversight, approval, authority, and accountability;
- classify actions as autonomous, monitored, prior approval, or prohibited;
- design decision-grade approval before consequential state change;
- recognize consent fatigue, automation bias, and ceremonial approval;
- separate requesting, recommending, approving, executing, monitoring, reviewing, and restoring roles;
- define timeout, absence, escalation, emergency, and fallback behavior;
- require evidence that human intervention is informed, timely, independent, and effective.

## Known limitations

- Action classifications depend on actual use, target, environment, data, volume, law, and consequence.
- “Human-in-the-loop” and “human-on-the-loop” labels are insufficient without operating details.
- No universal approval threshold, response time, or staffing model is prescribed.
- Human approval cannot repair missing authority, unsafe architecture, or ineffective target controls.
- The ORION case is fictional and simplified.
- Source status and applicability must be rechecked before production-facing use.
