# Module 12 Participant Workbook — Use-Case Classification and Risk Tiers

**Before you begin:** Complete this workbook using your own reasoning before reading the [Model Answer and Review Guide](./04_Module_02_Model_Answer_and_Review_Guide.md). Submitting the model answer as your own completed work does not satisfy the completion requirement in [Module 2's lesson](../02_Module_02_Use_Case_Classification_and_Risk_Tiers.md).

Use the fictional case in Section 8 of the lesson and your own Module 1 workbook. Do not introduce confidential information from your own employer; keep any real-world parallels abstract.

## Part 1 — Tier model

Define your own risk-tier model (you may adapt the lesson's four-tier illustration or design your own number of tiers). For each tier, state:

- its defining characteristics in your own words;
- the specific, checkable governance obligation attached to it — not a vague description like "extra care."

## Part 2 — Classify the case

For each of the five findings from Module 1's case (ORION, sales proposal drafting, benefits eligibility recommendation, customer-service analytics pilot, three disagreeing lists), assign a tier from your Part 1 model. For each assignment:

- name the classification factors (Section 2 of the lesson) that drove it;
- state whether the tier is final or provisional, and if provisional, name exactly what missing information would resolve it.

## Part 3 — Intake process

Design an intake process for new use cases entering the inventory. Name:

- who submits a classification;
- who decides the tier, and whether that person differs from the submitter when the tier reaches your model's highest levels;
- what independent check applies to a self-submitted tier near a tier boundary;
- what record is kept of the decision.

## Part 4 — Reclassification triggers

List at least five reclassification triggers. For each, state what evidence would show the trigger has occurred (not just that it theoretically could).

## Part 5 — Challenge a self-classification

Write a short scenario, in your own words, where a business owner has an incentive to under-classify a use case to avoid governance obligation. State exactly what in your Part 3 intake design would catch it, and what would happen if your design did not.

## Part 6 — Linking outward

Explain, in your own words:

- how a use case's tier here should affect which of Module 6's action classes eventually apply to it, using ORION as the example;
- how a use case's tier here should affect which of Module 9's lifecycle gates apply, and how rigorously.

## Part 7 — Executive summary

In no more than 200 words, summarize the tiering results to an executive audience: which use cases are provisional, which are high-impact, and what decision or resource you need next. Do not claim a tier is final when your own Part 2 marked it provisional.

## Part 8 — Defense

Prepare to defend, in writing or aloud:

- one tier assignment you expect a business owner to push back on, and why you would hold the line or revise it;
- one place your own tier model could itself be gamed by a submitter, and what you would change to close that gap.

## Completion note

Preserve this workbook as your own artifact. A completed workbook and defense — not the existence of this template — is what the Content Build Register and Progress Record treat as demonstrated learning.
