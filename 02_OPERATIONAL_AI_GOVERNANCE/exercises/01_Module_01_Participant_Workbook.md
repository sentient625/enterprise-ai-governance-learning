# Module 11 Participant Workbook — Enterprise AI Inventory and Ownership

**Before you begin:** Complete this workbook using your own reasoning before reading the [Model Answer and Review Guide](./02_Module_01_Model_Answer_and_Review_Guide.md). Submitting the model answer as your own completed work does not satisfy the completion requirement in [Module 11's lesson](../01_Module_01_AI_Inventory_and_Ownership.md).

Use the fictional case in Section 8 of the lesson. Do not introduce confidential information from your own employer; keep any real-world parallels abstract.

## Part 1 — Inventory schema

Using the minimum specification in Section 3 of the lesson, draft an inventory entry template with every field group. For each field group, write one sentence stating what would happen to a governance decision later if that field were left blank.

## Part 2 — Populate the case

For each of the five findings in Section 8 (ORION, sales proposal drafting, benefits eligibility recommendation, customer-service analytics pilot, three disagreeing business-unit lists), populate as much of your Part 1 template as the case facts support. Mark every field you cannot populate as "unknown," not as a guess.

## Part 3 — Ownership assignment

For each of the five findings, name:

- the accountable business owner (or state explicitly that none currently exists and who should be asked to accept the role);
- the technical owner;
- the governance/control owner;
- one specific reason the current state is, or is not, an acceptable ownership gap to leave open while it is being resolved.

## Part 4 — Detection plan

Design a detection approach for:

1. Shadow AI similar to the sales-proposal case.
2. Embedded vendor AI similar to the benefits-eligibility case.

For each, name at least two independent data sources you would reconcile, and state one way each source could still miss the use case.

## Part 5 — Reconciliation cadence

Propose a reconciliation cadence and trigger set for the enterprise inventory. Justify the cadence by the kind of change it is meant to catch, not by a default calendar interval.

## Part 6 — Linking to Module 9

Explain, in your own words, the difference between what belongs in this enterprise-wide inventory and what belongs in an individual agent's Module 9 lifecycle record. Using ORION as the example, state exactly what the enterprise-inventory entry should contain and what it should instead reference rather than duplicate.

## Part 7 — Executive summary

In no more than 200 words, summarize the state of the enterprise AI inventory to an executive audience: what is known, what is still unknown, what the immediate ownership gaps are, and what you would ask for next. Do not claim the inventory is complete.

## Part 8 — Defense

Prepare to defend, in writing or aloud:

- one use case in the case where you would resist a request to "just leave it running until the review is done," and why;
- one place where your own workbook still contains an unresolved unknown, and what evidence would resolve it.

## Completion note

Preserve this workbook as your own artifact. A completed workbook and defense — not the existence of this template — is what the Content Build Register and Progress Record treat as demonstrated learning.
