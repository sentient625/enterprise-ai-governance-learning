# Module 15 Participant Workbook — Monitoring and Change Governance

**Before you begin:** Complete this workbook using your own reasoning before reading the [Model Answer and Review Guide](./10_Module_05_Model_Answer_and_Review_Guide.md). Submitting the model answer as your own completed work does not satisfy the completion requirement in [Module 5's lesson](../05_Module_05_Monitoring_and_Change_Governance.md).

Use the fictional case in Section 7 of the lesson and your own Module 1–4 workbooks. Do not introduce confidential information from your own employer; keep any real-world parallels abstract.

## Part 1 — Monitoring plan

Using the minimum specification in Section 2 of the lesson, draft a monitoring plan for the benefits eligibility recommendation use case as it returns to constrained operation after Module 4's remediation. Include at least four signals, each with a definition, baseline, threshold, owner, and response deadline.

## Part 2 — Leading indicator versus incident

For two of your Part 1 signals, write a scenario where that signal crosses its threshold. For each scenario, decide whether it is a leading indicator or a confirmed incident, state which governing response applies (Section 3), and name who makes that call.

## Part 3 — Change envelope

Define a change envelope for this use case: what an owner may change without triggering a full reassessment, and what falls outside it. Justify at least one boundary you drew — why is that specific change inside or outside the envelope?

## Part 4 — Vendor disclosure

Draft a vendor-disclosure requirement for this use case's contract, per Section 5. State what happens if the vendor does not comply, and what substitute signal your Part 1 monitoring plan would rely on if disclosure fails.

## Part 5 — The applied scenario

Using Section 7's six-months-later scenario (an undisclosed vendor model update detected through override-rate drift), walk through your own monitoring plan from Part 1: which signal would have caught this, how quickly, and what would happen next under your Part 3 change envelope.

## Part 6 — Linking outward

Explain, in your own words, how this module's monitoring plan differs from Module 4's TEVV plan, and how a leading indicator from this module's monitoring should connect to Module 8's suspension and response process once it becomes a confirmed incident.

## Part 7 — Executive summary

In no more than 200 words, summarize to an executive audience what monitoring is now in place for this use case, what would trigger a response, and what remains a gap (for example, an untested vendor-disclosure obligation). Do not claim full coverage if your own plan leaves something unmonitored.

## Part 8 — Defense

Prepare to defend, in writing or aloud:

- one signal in your plan you expect someone to argue is unnecessary, and why you would keep it;
- one place where your own change envelope could be gamed by defining a change narrowly enough to stay inside it, and how you would close that gap.

## Completion note

Preserve this workbook as your own artifact. A completed workbook and defense — not the existence of this template — is what the Content Build Register and Progress Record treat as demonstrated learning.
