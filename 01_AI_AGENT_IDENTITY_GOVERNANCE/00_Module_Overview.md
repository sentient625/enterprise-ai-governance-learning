# Priority 1 — AI-Agent Identity, Security, and Governance

## Objective

Develop the executive and program-level fluency required to lead the governed adoption of AI agents that can use enterprise identities, tools, data, and privileged access.

This priority does not attempt to make a learner an AI engineer, IAM architect, or product administrator. It develops the ability to:

- Frame the business proposition.
- Identify the acting identities and authority chain.
- Challenge credential, privilege, tool, and delegation design.
- Establish human decision boundaries.
- Require decision-grade evidence.
- Define suspension, revocation, fallback, and retirement.
- Connect operational evidence to continued investment.

## Suggested working folders

Create these only as work is produced:

- `exercises/` — short daily exercises and diagrams.
- `completed_work/` — populated lifecycle, case responses, and decision memorandum.

Git does not preserve empty folders, so they will appear with the first completed artifact.

## Completion evidence

- Populated lifecycle artifact.
- Completed case workbook.
- One-page decision memorandum.
- Fifteen-minute oral defense or a written defense covering the same questions.
- Interview-ready explanation of how to lead a privileged-agent program.
- Progress Record updated from In progress to Demonstrated.

## First action

For Module 1, read the lesson, complete the participant workbook without the model answer, and then compare and defend the analysis. Record demonstrated learning only after the artifact and defense exist.

## Positioning boundary

A completed module supports this statement:

> I can lead the program that establishes how enterprise AI agents are identified, authorized, constrained, monitored, audited, suspended, and retired.

It does not support a claim that a learner personally engineered the underlying identity, token, agent, or PAM platform.
