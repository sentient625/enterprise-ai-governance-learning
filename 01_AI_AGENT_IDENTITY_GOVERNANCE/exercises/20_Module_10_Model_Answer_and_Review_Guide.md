# Module 10 Model Answer and Review Guide

**Version:** 1.0 | **Status:** Accepted for public learning use

Use after completing the [participant workbook](./19_Module_10_Participant_Workbook.md). This is one defensible educational response to the fictional [ORION case](../03_Regulated_Enterprise_Case_Workbook.md), not a completed learner submission, real authorization, or tested production design. Different recommendations can succeed if their boundaries and evidence are defensible.

## 1. Decision dossier — model observations

| Claim | Evidence state | Decision consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Lower restoration time and after-hours effort | Sponsor hypothesis; baseline incomplete | Do not recognize savings; design a comparator and full-effort measurement |
| Registered service principal establishes attribution | Only part of the proposed chain; shared legacy and human-session paths remain | Resolve or exclude those paths before dependent activation |
| Approval click makes production safe | Proposal omits relevant goal, authority and consequence context | Require meaningful decision content, timing and authorized approver |
| Specialist helper remains within bounds | Assumption, not demonstrated enforcement | Exclude helper execution or prove delegated limits and attribution |
| Logs reconstruct an action | Multiple records lack common correlation and aligned clocks | Do not claim complete provenance or known approval timing |
| Agent service can be stopped safely | Independent stop and continuity are requirements, not successful tests | Require evidence covering credentials, tools, queues, in-flight and downstream work |

The appendix should reference the learner's identity chain, bounded authority statement, tool/context matrix, human decision matrix, evidence specification, response runbook and lifecycle/inventory record. “See Module 8” is not a reference to a completed learner runbook. An artifact may be complete as an analysis while explicitly documenting that implementation evidence is absent.

## 2. Compare options — model recommendation

**Approve as designed:** Unsupported. The proposed IT/OT and privileged scope exceeds available control and benefit evidence.

**Approve with constraints:** Potentially defensible only if exclusions create a separable scope and preactivation gates are enforced. Renaming the same cross-environment architecture “constrained” does not solve the problem.

**Bounded pilot:** Preferred as a proposed experiment: prepare an isolated nonproduction recommendation-only pilot using controlled or synthetic data. Hold activation until named authorities accept the gates. No production/OT execution, shared or human-session credentials, autonomous privilege grants, agent-controlled break-glass, destructive actions or unbounded helpers. A later bounded UAT execution experiment would be a separate authorization.

**Defer/do not authorize:** Also defensible. If isolation, meaningful value measurement, ownership or viable fallback cannot be established, defer even the narrower pilot. The counterargument to our preferred choice is that a recommendation-only experiment may not test the sponsor's restoration benefit hypothesis. It tests a narrower information/triage proposition, and cannot justify privileged expansion by itself.

Preparation funding, design acceptance, operating authorization and activation need separate records. The original case supplies no accepted combined decision owner; an assignment is a gate, not a fact to invent.

## 3. Conditions and value experiment — illustrative proposal

All owners below are proposed role assignments; named holders, delegated authority, acceptance and numeric limits must be resolved before activation. No row is closed by this answer.

| ID/type | Proposed owner → acceptance authority | Required proof and due event | Effect if unmet |
|---|---|---|---|
| G1 preactivation: ownership and baseline | Program/business leads → designated operating authority and required control owners | Named accountable holders, accepted duties, exact baseline, action/data/tool inventory, expiry and resource commitments before activation | Hold all pilot activation |
| G2 preactivation: identity and isolation | IAM/technical leads → qualified security/architecture acceptance owners | Unique scoped pilot identity; controlled data access; negative tests for production/OT, write tools, shared identities and forbidden delegation before activation | Hold affected access; redesign or exclude path |
| G3 preactivation: human use and evidence | Operations plus records/privacy leads → assigned control authorities | Reviewable recommendations; humans remain responsible for subsequent actions; reconstructable requests/outputs and approvals where applicable; restricted evidence handling before activation | Hold dependent workflow |
| G4 preactivation: stop and fallback | Operations/IAM/technical leads → designated response/release authorities | Demonstration on pilot baseline of independent stop, outstanding-work reconciliation, credential/tool restrictions, evidence preservation and manual continuity before activation | Hold activation; stopping service alone is insufficient |
| G5 preactivation: experimental design | Business/finance/operations leads → sponsor and operating authority within their remits | Accepted comparator, task population, period/sample plan, benefit/quality thresholds, full cost ceiling, volume/concurrency and duration before activation | No open-ended or unmeasurable pilot |
| O1 operating: boundary/evidence health | Duty operations lead → delegated suspension authority | Monitor actual scope and evidence; suspend affected activity at boundary breach or loss of required evidence | Contain, preserve evidence, reconcile effects; no automatic restart |
| O2 operating: value and expiry | Business/operational owners → authorized review body | Review measured value at agreed checkpoint; permission ends at approved expiry unless renewed | Stop or restrict through assigned enforcement path; reassess or retire |
| X1 expansion | Program lead coordinates → authorities for the proposed environment/action | New scope-specific tests, value case, risk acceptance, staffing, meaningful approvals and restoration proof before expansion | No production/OT or privilege expansion |

For G5, compare matched eligible triage tasks under the manual process and recommendation assistance. Measure total elapsed time, human minutes including review/correction, error/rework rate and operating cost. Protect against selection bias by defining task eligibility before observing results. Count rejected or failed recommendations. Have business and operations owners agree sample sufficiency and thresholds; the case provides no defensible numeric return target.

If review effort exceeds saved effort, reassess usefulness rather than reporting draft-generation speed as business benefit. Even a favorable result does not establish cash savings, privileged remediation performance or OT safety. The original 90-day request is not automatically adopted for this narrower experiment.

## 4. Illustrative one-page memorandum

The following is a model of the requested content, approximately one page when conventionally formatted. Appendix references refer to the analysis above; this is not a signed decision.

**To:** Designated ORION decision authority, to be assigned, with business, operations and qualified control owners

**From:** Program lead, fictional case exercise

**Subject:** Prepare a bounded nonproduction ORION experiment; hold activation pending evidence

**Decision requested.** Select the bounded-pilot option. Authorize preparation of an isolated, recommendation-only nonproduction experiment if the sponsor accepts a defined preparation budget. This recommendation grants no operational authority. Before activation, assign a decision holder with documented authority and obtain required security, architecture, IAM, operations and records/privacy concurrence. The current proposal's production and protected-OT activities remain outside the recommended scope.

**Business proposition.** ORION may improve triage and reduce human effort, but the case does not establish a baseline, net benefit or realized savings. Compare eligible tasks with the manual process and consider conventional process improvement. Count review, corrections, failed outputs, monitoring, provider costs and maintenance. Accept the task population, sample plan, quality/benefit thresholds, cost ceiling and duration before activation under G5. This experiment cannot demonstrate privileged restoration or OT benefit.

**Evidence and uncertainty.** The proposal includes shared legacy credentials and a human-session path, incomplete approval context, mismatched evidence and an unproven independent stop. Proposed controls are not successful tests. We cannot establish a complete attribution chain, approval timing, outcome reconstruction or safe interruption from the supplied dossier. Supplier assurance or a funding decision would not close those gaps.

**Scope.** Permit only preparation now. The eventual pilot would use a unique bounded identity, controlled or synthetic data, named read tools and reviewed recommendations in an isolated nonproduction environment. Exclude target execution, production/OT access, shared or human-session credentials, autonomous access grants, agent break-glass and unbounded helper delegation. Numeric operating limits and expiry must be agreed before activation. Human use of recommendations still requires accountable review and appropriate information controls.

**Conditions.** G1 establishes the baseline and accepted ownership. G2 requires tested identity and environmental boundaries. G3 requires meaningful human use and reconstructable, protected evidence. G4 requires demonstrated independent containment, outstanding-work reconciliation and viable manual continuity. G5 establishes a measurable, bounded experiment. Named acceptance authorities must record proof before the release authority activates the matching baseline. No gate is presently demonstrated closed. If separation or useful measurement is infeasible, return with a deferral recommendation.

**Stop and next decision.** Delegate suspension authority to named operations responders before activation. Boundary violations or loss of necessary evidence trigger containment of affected activity, preservation and reconciliation; credential, queue and downstream effects require explicit handling. Restoration requires a new accepted decision and relevant proof. O2 ends permission at expiry and brings value evidence to the agreed review. X1 requires separate scope-specific authorization for any expansion. Do not carry this pilot's approval into production or OT.

**Record status:** Recommendation only; decision holder, concurrence, accepted evidence and authorization date pending.

## 5. Briefing and defense — expected reasoning

A model briefing:

> I recommend preparing a bounded nonproduction experiment and holding activation until its gates are met. ORION may reduce triage effort, but we have no established net-benefit baseline. The proposed privileged IT and OT scope also has unresolved attribution, approval, evidence and interruption gaps. Funding preparation does not grant runtime authority. The narrower experiment would use controlled data and reviewed recommendations, with no target execution or unbounded helpers. Named owners must demonstrate isolation, traceable evidence, independent containment and human continuity, and agree cost, duration and value thresholds. If they cannot, defer. At the review, compare full human effort and quality against the manual process. Favorable results support another decision; they do not authorize privileged expansion. I need assignment of the decision authority and acceptance owners, then dated proof against each gate.

For the production-restart trace, distinguish the initiating human/business request, ORION runtime identity, token/credential used by the tool, tool permissions, target enforcement, approver and resulting state. The shared legacy or human-session path can break attribution. An authenticated caller does not prove delegated business authority. The incomplete approval display and unaligned timestamps prevent a claim of timely informed approval. Tool success does not establish the final server state. This proposed action remains excluded.

Answers to reviewer challenges should identify: separate funding and operational authorities; the decisive gap tied to the chosen scope; confidentiality and reliance risk in read-only assistance; full effort and cost as a falsification test; qualified specialist decisions; and evidence that would change the recommendation. A confident refusal to reconsider is not stronger than a reasoned revision.

### Inject responses

**A — Expansion pressure:** The requested OT restart is outside scope. Hold that agent action and use the separately authorized human operational process if action is needed. Obtain OT-specific authority and evidence for any future agent proposal. Urgency and sponsor support do not amend the boundary.

**B — Evidence conflict:** Outcome and approval timing remain unresolved. If this occurs during an authorized hypothetical operation, hold further dependent actions under assigned response authority, preserve records, reconcile target state through trusted observation and examine clock/correlation limits. If still preactivation, keep the gate open. Do not relabel a tool result as a confirmed target outcome or assume approval was late. Restoration needs accepted proof.

**C — Weak value:** This hypothetical evidence weakens the benefit claim. At the value checkpoint, constrain, redesign or end the experiment according to the agreed thresholds and decision rights. Include correction effort and failed tasks. Faster draft generation alone does not justify continued spending or expansion. Poor value alone does not prove a security incident.

## 6. Revision and interview translation

The review log must show the original answer, challenge, evidence, resulting change or reason unchanged, and remaining uncertainty. A learner might clarify that preparation is permitted while activation is held, or narrow a value claim from restoration-time improvement to triage-effort testing. Changes must be reflected consistently in the memo, conditions and briefing.

A credible interview translation:

> In a fictional ORION capstone, I developed and defended a bounded-pilot recommendation. I linked the business hypothesis to identity, authority, human approval, evidence and recovery requirements, separated funding from activation, and specified accountable proof gates. My contribution is program leadership: coordinating qualified owners, exposing unresolved decisions and maintaining conditions for operation and expansion. This learning case does not claim that I engineered the controls or delivered measured savings in a live deployment.

## 7. Educational scoring rubric

Score each dimension 0–2: **0** absent, materially wrong or unsupported; **1** addressed but incomplete or weakly traceable; **2** specific, consistent and evidence-linked. Maximum 20; target at least 16, no zero, and no unresolved critical failure. These are original instructional thresholds.

| Dimension | A score of 2 requires |
|---|---|
| 1. Dossier and traceability | Facts, assumptions, proposals and unknowns separated; original lifecycle/case and prior artifacts referenced |
| 2. Choice and alternatives | Four options compared fairly; counterargument and change-of-mind evidence stated |
| 3. Business value | Comparator, full cost/effort, quality, bounded measurement and falsification without invented returns |
| 4. Identity and scope | Explicit environments/actions/identities/tools/data/delegation limits and proof of enforcement required |
| 5. Authority and human decisions | Funding/authorization/activation separated; deciding roles, concurrence and meaningful approvals identified |
| 6. Evidence and uncertainty | Attempt versus outcome, timing gaps, provenance and protected evidence handling maintained |
| 7. Response and continuity | Independent containment, outstanding work, fallback and separately authorized restoration |
| 8. Conditions and lifecycle | Owners, acceptance evidence, deadlines, consequence, expiry and separate expansion decision |
| 9. Communication and defense | Consistent concise memo/briefing; transaction trace; reasoned challenge and inject response |
| 10. Revision and positioning | Review record, justified revision or retention, qualified-owner boundaries and truthful interview translation |

Critical failures requiring revision regardless of score:

- Inventing successful tests, signed approvals, realized benefits or known outcomes/timing.
- Allowing activation with unclosed required gates, or equating sponsor funding with operational authority.
- Allowing production/OT or other out-of-scope operation through an undefined exception.
- Treating stop-service alone as proven containment, or permitting restoration without required evidence and authority.
- Treating copied model content as learner work, or claiming certification, independent review or real implementation results from this exercise.

A well-supported deferral may score as highly as the model pilot recommendation. Record reviewer identity, date, assessment method, dimension scores, critical findings and reassessment. Label self-review accurately. The rubric supports review of learning evidence; it does not itself update the Progress Record or grant production authority.
