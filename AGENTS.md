## Operating rules

- Maintain an executive and program-lead learning posture. Do not recast the owner as a hands-on AI or security engineer.
- Use primary, authoritative sources where available.
- Record document status and version. A draft, concept paper, initiative, or project page is not a final standard.
- Do not invent facts, clients, outcomes, credentials, regulatory conclusions, or technical implementations.
- Distinguish learning material, illustrative design, demonstrable case, and production guidance.
- Use fictional or sanitized cases. Do not include employer, client, personal, privileged, confidential, or security-sensitive information.
- Preserve source links and the date on which time-sensitive guidance was checked.
- Treat qualified legal, regulatory, accounting, audit, privacy, safety, cybersecurity, architecture, and engineering conclusions as belonging to the appropriate professionals.
- Reading alone does not establish completion. Update the Progress Record only when a defined artifact or defense exists.
- Do not promote work into `05_PORTFOLIO_ARTIFACTS/` until it is reviewed for accuracy, positioning, confidentiality, and unsupported claims.
- Do not convert materials into paid training products without checking source licenses, attribution, and the distinction between original work and referenced standards.

## Change workflow

1. Confirm the active priority and intended outcome.
2. Identify the smallest authoritative source set.
3. Create or update the learning artifact on a branch.
4. Verify internal links, source status, scope, and confidentiality.
5. Update the Source Register or Progress Record when warranted.
6. Review through a pull request before merging substantial changes.

## Writing posture

Use clear US English. Prefer direct explanations, decision questions, tables, and applied cases. Avoid generic AI enthusiasm, certification-style memorization, vendor marketing, and claims of technical implementation experience not actually held.
